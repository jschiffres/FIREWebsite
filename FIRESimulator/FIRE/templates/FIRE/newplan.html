Tips

  • Be honest about your current age. Every projection starts from it, so even a year's difference shifts your results.
  • Pick a retirement age you could live with. It's just a starting point. You can change it on the dashboard at any time and instantly see the effect.
  • Aim for a range, not a single date. Try a target a few years earlier and later once your plan is built.
  • Name your plan. A clear name such as "Baseline" or "Move abroad" makes plans easier to compare later.
  • Enter pre-tax amounts. By default your taxes are estimated for you from your filing status and state. Turn that off to enter your own tax rate for each income.
  • Add every source. Use Salary for your job (with a yearly bonus if it's reliable), Recurring for rental income, pensions or Social Security, and One-time for inheritances or sales.
  • Plan for raises. "Yearly Change" models raises or declines. 2-4% is common, roughly inflation plus a little.
  • Set Start and Stop Ages. Give contract work or a pension its real dates so you don't overstate future earnings.
  • Stay conservative. Leave out bonuses and windfalls you can't count on.
  • Check a few months of statements. Most people underestimate spending. Your real number drives your Magic Number.
  • Include the irregular costs. Annual insurance, travel, gifts, car repairs and home maintenance add up. Divide by 12 if needed.
  • Remember life changes. Use Recurring expenses with Start and Stop Ages for things like a mortgage or childcare, and One-time expenses for big purchases.
  • Plan for healthcare. It's often the biggest new expense when you retire before Medicare age.
  • Lower spending helps twice. It lets you save more now and reduces how much you need in retirement.
  • Simple is the fastest route: one pool of savings growing at a single rate.
  • Recommended fills accounts in a tax-smart order: 401(k) up to the employer match, then HSA, 401(k) and IRA. Each account is optional.
  • Custom (Premium) lets you add several accounts of any type, order them by priority, and choose how savings fill each one (limit, fixed $, percent, or employer match), mark old accounts as dormant, and add a high yield savings account for surplus savings. At least one account is required.
  • Always capture the employer match. It's an immediate, guaranteed return.
  • Use realistic Yearly Growth. Long-run stock returns of about 5-7% after inflation are a common planning assumption. Cash and bonds grow more slowly.
  • Include what you already have. Current Value is added to your starting net worth.

These are general educational tips, not financial advice.
